Most portfolio case studies are screenshots of a spreadsheet someone can no longer open. These are not. Every figure on every page — the discount rates, the ten-thousand-trial simulation, the knapsack that picks nine initiatives out of twelve — is computed in your browser when the page loads, from models I wrote from scratch with no charting library and no framework.

That is partly a craft decision and partly an honest one. If a case study cannot survive you moving its assumptions, it was never analysis. It was a conclusion with decoration.

A note on the data

Every company in these three pieces is an illustrative composite, built so its operating profile sits inside publicly documented ranges for its industry. I chose that deliberately over the alternative, which is to attach invented financials to a real company's name and hope nobody checks.

What is real is everything built on top: the discounting, the correlation structure in the simulation, the debt schedule, the MECE decomposition that sums exactly to the observed change, and the dynamic program that solves the allocation to optimality rather than approximating it. Those are the parts of the work that transfer to a real dataset — and the parts worth judging.

Each case also carries a section on where its own model is weakest, because an analysis that only argues its own case is a pitch.
